Comparing the Gear

We use Comparatives to compare two objects, and Superlatives to describe the extreme limit of a group (the maximum or minimum).

Adjective Type Comparative (A vs B) Superlative (The extreme)
Short (fast, heavy) Add -er + than
heavier than		 the + Add -est
the heaviest
Long (expensive, difficult) more + adj + than
more expensive than		 the most + adj
the most expensive
Irregulars (good / bad) better than / worse than the best / the worst
⚠️ Spelling Rule:
If a short adjective ends in -y (like heavy), change the 'y' to 'i' before adding the ending: heavier / the heaviest.

B Kettlebell vs. Dumbbell

Compare the two pieces of equipment. Complete the sentences with the Comparative form of the adjective + than.

1. The black barbell is (heavy) the red dumbbells.
2. CrossFit is (difficult) running on a treadmill.
3. This carbon fiber jump rope is (fast) the plastic one.
4. For my back injuries, swimming is (good) lifting weights.

C Pushing to the Limit

Describe the extremes of the gym. Complete the sentences with the Superlative form of the adjective in brackets. (Don't forget "the"!).

1. That 100kg iron ball is (heavy) kettlebell in the gym.
2. Doing 100 burpees is (exhausting) exercise in today's routine.
3. The wooden box is (cheap) piece of equipment we have.
4. Yesterday I dropped a weight on my foot. It was (bad) day of my life!

D The WOD Briefing

Read the coach's message. Fill in the gaps using the comparative or superlative form of the words in brackets.

"Okay team, today's Workout of the Day is (1. hard) yesterday's routine. We are using barbells. Please remember that safety is (2. important) rule here. If the 50kg bar is too heavy for you, choose a (3. light) one! Do not try to lift (4. heavy) weight if your technique is bad. A good technique is always (5. good) lifting a lot of kilos with a bad posture."
